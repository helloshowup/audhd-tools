Constants
Collapsed by default on purpose. Clinical notes names the tracker-rumination loop as the main risk of a tool like this, and the assessment recommends reducing "just right" checking. Log for seven days before you move anything. A single day is not a signal.

Budget

Base budget is in felt spoons, the same unit as the buttons you tap. Default 7, which is the top of your own morning-estimate range. The old default of 12 told you that you had roughly double the capacity you report having.
Sleep penalty defaults to 0.03 per hour short, down from 0.08. When sleep is intact but fatigue remains elevated, sleep should not be the heaviest penalty in the daily budget.
Yesterday now carries in both directions. Debt fraction 0.3 means going 4 over yesterday costs you 1.2 today. Credit fraction 1.0 means every spoon you did not spend yesterday is added to today, uncapped, so today's budget can read well above the base of 7 after a quiet day. Set credit to 0 to go back to debt-only. Nothing is stored: both are recomputed from your raw entries on every load, so changing either number rewrites every day in the calibration table below.
Both are off because a phone cannot measure either overnight, and a daytime resting-HR rise could be posture (your POTS toggle) or the blood-pressure question in your report rather than depletion.

Medication correction


The correction is now a curve over time since dose, not a flat number, because the heart-rate effect lags the drug. Plasma d-amphetamine peaks around 3.8 hours, but the pulse increase peaks 8 to 12.5 hours post-dose. A correction that peaked at chemical Tmax would over-correct your mornings and under-correct your evenings, which is exactly backwards for a early morning dose and an afternoon dip.
Defaults: 50 mg, peak 5.2 bpm at 10 hours, which puts the implied daily mean on the 4.2 bpm reported for 50 mg in Adler et al. 2009 (30 mg +2.8, 50 mg +4.2, 70 mg +5.2). The adult meta-analytic resting figure is +5.7 bpm (Mick et al. 2013). The old flat 8 bpm is only defensible as a late-day peak at 70 mg. Anchoring instead on the observed late-day peak (9.9 bpm at 70 mg, scaled to your dose) would put the peak nearer 8 and the mean nearer 6.5. The two anchors disagree and only your own washout data can settle which is right for you. house rule Magnitude for you needs a washout comparison of medicated against non-medicated days before it means anything.

Computed cost

house rule Percent of heart-rate reserve is validated against oxygen-uptake reserve for physical exertion only. There is no validated extension of it to cognitive or emotional load, cognitive load moves heart rate by single-digit bpm against tens for exercise, and no measured cardiovascular signature of masking exists. The head dial and the condition multipliers are constructs with a plausible rationale, not measurements. They are the honest part of this app as long as they are labelled.
Multipliers no longer multiply out unchecked. They are sorted by size and each further one contributes a tapered share of its excess, then the total is capped. Four tags on a sample busy-day entry produced 2.53x under the old rule, inflating the computed figure above the felt scale.
Suggested head scale is read off your own entries that carry both a felt cost and a computed one. It needs several paired entries before it means anything.
